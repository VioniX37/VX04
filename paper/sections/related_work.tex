\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{AutoML systems.} Auto-sklearn \citep{feurer2015autosklearn} and AutoGluon \citep{erickson2020autogluon} search over pipelines and ensembles with fixed search spaces; Auto-sklearn warm-starts the search from configurations that worked on datasets with similar meta-features. Multi-fidelity methods such as successive halving and Hyperband \citep{jamieson2016successive,li2018hyperband} allocate compute to promising configurations using cheap low-fidelity evaluations.

\paragraph{LLM agents for data science.} DS-Agent \citep{guo2024dsagent} uses case-based reasoning over Kaggle solutions; SELA \citep{chi2024sela} combines LLM agents with Monte Carlo tree search over executed experiments. AutoML-Agent \citep{trirat2025automlagent} covers the full pipeline with retrieval-augmented planning and pseudo-execution.

\paragraph{Memory and experience in LLM agents.} Reflexion \citep{shinn2023reflexion} stores verbal reflections on failed attempts and conditions later attempts on them; ExpeL \citep{zhao2024expel} extracts insights and successful trajectories from past tasks and recalls them for new ones. Our experience memory is closer to meta-learning warm starts than to free-text reflection: it stores \emph{measured} outcomes (observed scores of every plan at every fidelity) and concrete error$\to$fix pairs, keyed by dataset meta-features rather than by text similarity.

\paragraph{Calibration of LLM predictions.} LLMs can be reasonably calibrated about their own answers in some settings \citep{kadavath2022know}, but verbalised confidence is often over-confident \citep{xiong2024uncertainty}. Pseudo-execution asks for something harder: a numeric prediction of a model's validation score on unseen data. We are not aware of prior measurements of this quantity in agentic AutoML.

\paragraph{Our position.} We keep AutoML-Agent's agentic planning, replace its unverified pseudo-execution with multi-fidelity \emph{grounding} borrowed from classical AutoML, and add a memory of \emph{measured} outcomes. Because selection then rests on measurements, we also guard the measurements themselves: a pre-training audit removes leaking columns \citep{kaufman2012leakage}, the test split is never used for selection, and served models replay the exact training-time feature preparation \citep{sculley2015debt}.
